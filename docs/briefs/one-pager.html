Product overview

A memory for the whole manuscript

Bookinator is a local editorial workbench that reads a manuscript chapter by chapter, preserves the evidence, connects observations across the book, and turns the result into reports another person can inspect.

0.1.0Holy Crap, that Worked

The first working milestone proved the complete loop on a real book: import, analyze, reconcile, explore, and share.

The editorial problem

A manuscript can make a promise in one chapter and return to it hundreds of pages later. Summaries smooth over small details, while ordinary search cannot reliably connect changed names, related events, missing appearances, or a question with its eventual answer.

Analysis creates the evidence

Bookinator imports PDF, DOCX, EPUB, TXT, and Markdown. It proposes the chapter map, then saves chapter summaries, evidence-rich dossiers, emotion scores, editorial tags, and Smells. Whole-book rollups reconcile the completed chapter work.

Inferences cross the book

Connections groups entities, locations, and time observations across chapters. Questions & Payoffs follows narrative questions from where they arise to possible or resolved answers. These are inspectable algorithms, not hidden report magic.

Explore makes the result useful

Editors can sort indexes, inspect aliases and close names, see presence and absence by chapter, follow intersections, review an emotion map scaled by chapter length, and return to saved evidence and run details.

Share without rerunning

Bookinator exports a branded editorial PDF and a model-free interactive HTML report. Both use saved analysis. The interactive report opens offline on an ordinary computer and excludes the source manuscript.

Private and inspectable

The loopback server, book projects, model output, and ordinary analysis stay on the user’s computer. Local language models run through Ollama. Status, model identity, timing, failures, and retries remain visible.

Current boundary: Bookinator produces editorial leads, not proof. Grouping can be wrong, timeline observations remain rough, source precision varies by artifact, and long local model jobs can take substantial time. Reviewer annotations, revision comparison, and a deeper continuity engine remain future work.
