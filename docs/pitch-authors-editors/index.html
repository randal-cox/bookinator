Bookinator · 20-minute overview

Find what the author forgot

A free, local manuscript intelligence tool that remembers what a book promises—and shows its work.

A manuscript can be locally coherent and globally wrong. A vanished promise, a changed age, or a timeline that no longer fits may be hundreds of pages from where it began. Bookinator is built to help authors and editors find those failures without sending an unpublished book to a hosted service. Today I will show the problem it is designed around, the workflow, its boundaries, and why I intend to keep it free.
01

The editing problem

A book is longer than any working memory

Characters, facts, promises, mysteries, objects, and timelines accumulate. The defect often appears far from the sentence that created it.

450
By page 450, the text may depend on a tiny fact established on page 30. Authors know this intuitively and build notebooks, spreadsheets, search rituals, and margin notes. Editors do the same. These methods are valuable, but the work of connecting distant evidence remains manual and fragile.
02

The failure mode

Most AI reads passages. Books create obligations.

  • A fact must remain true—or visibly change
  • A promise must be paid off—or deliberately deferred
  • A mystery must preserve what each character knows
  • A timeline must survive the whole manuscript
A summary is not an obligation ledger. It compresses away exactly the small details that later matter. Retrieval alone is not enough either: you cannot reliably ask for the right passage when you do not yet know what has gone wrong. Bookinator treats narrative facts and promises as durable objects that persist across the scan.
03

The product

A second memory for the whole manuscript

Bookinator reads locally, builds evidence-linked dossiers, and returns findings to the exact source passages that support them.

The goal is not to replace judgment. It is to make judgment better supplied with evidence.
Think of Bookinator as a second memory, not a second author. It does not decide whether an ambiguity is artful or accidental. It keeps track of claims and narrative obligations, identifies tensions, and brings the relevant passages together so a human can decide.
04

How it works

Import → inspect → verify

ImportPDF becomes readable canonical text
InspectChapters create facts, entities, events, and promises
VerifyFindings link back to original passages
The workflow begins with an ordinary manuscript PDF. Bookinator extracts and reflows it into readable text, detects chapters, and lets the user correct structure. Local models then build chapter-level records and book-level hypotheses. Every useful finding should lead back to source evidence; if it cannot, it is not ready to trust.
05

The durable model

Keep the small things alive

Evidence
Obligation
Finding
This is the core design choice. Evidence anchors an obligation: a fact, promise, relationship, mystery, timeline claim, or unresolved thread. Later evidence may satisfy it, revise it, or contradict it. Findings are produced from those relationships and remain inspectable. The system should never make a confident accusation from an untraceable summary.
06

Use case · author

Revision without losing the invisible threads

After a structural edit, rescan the manuscript and ask what changed, what broke, and what no longer pays off.

  • Continuity after moving or cutting chapters
  • Character knowledge and motivation
  • Promises introduced but never resolved
  • Repeated exposition and accidental redundancy
Authors are often most vulnerable after a major revision. Moving one chapter can break what a character knows; deleting a scene can orphan a payoff; changing a backstory can leave old facts behind. Bookinator should make the second-order effects of revision easier to see.
07

Use case · developmental editor

Arrive with a map, not a verdict

  • Trace plot and relationship arcs
  • Separate deliberate ambiguity from missing setup
  • Compare distant passages side by side
  • Turn a hunch into a cited editorial question
For developmental editors, the value is not automatic diagnosis. It is faster orientation and better questions. A finding should sound like: “These passages appear to disagree; is the change intentional?” That posture protects the editor’s authority and the author’s intent.
08

Use case · copy and continuity

Search catches words. Bookinator can track meaning.

Search findsBookinator should connect
Every “blue coat”The coat was destroyed before it reappears
Every dateTravel and elapsed time cannot fit
Every nameA character knows information they never learned
Repeated phrasesThe same explanation is doing duplicate work
Traditional search remains indispensable. Bookinator is useful where the contradiction is semantic rather than lexical. It can gather passages that refer to the same object under different names, compare elapsed time across chapters, or trace who learned what and when.

Privacy

The unpublished manuscript stays on your computer

Bookinator uses local files and local models. There is no required cloud account and no manuscript upload.

Unpublished writing is unusually sensitive. Local-first is not a decorative feature; it is part of the product contract. The project library, extracted text, model output, and provenance stay on the user’s machine. The tradeoff is honest: local setup and model performance depend on the hardware available.
10

Trust

Every finding should answer: “Why do you think that?”

Source passages, model identity, prompt version, run history, and uncertainty turn a claim into something an editor can inspect.

Trust here comes from inspectability rather than confidence styling. Bookinator retains which model ran, what generation produced an output, and where the supporting text came from. Failed attempts are diagnostic evidence, not something to hide. A polished sentence with no source trail is less useful than a cautious question with citations.
11

What it is not

No “fix my book” button

  • Not a ghostwriter
  • Not an originality score
  • Not a substitute for an editor
  • Not proof that a finding is an error
  • Not a reason to surrender authorial intent
There is a seductive but bad product nearby: upload a novel, receive a score and a pile of authoritative rewrites. I do not want to build that. Bookinator should preserve authorship and strengthen the human editorial relationship. Its job is remembering, connecting, and citing—not flattening style into a model’s preference.
12

Free forever

This is a public instrument, not a funnel

No premium tier. No paid institutional control plane. No manuscript-data business model.

  • Free to use
  • Local and inspectable
  • Portable book projects
  • GitHub-visible code and contribution paths
  • Double-clickable macOS, Windows, and Linux launchers
The project can be ambitious without becoming a startup. “Free forever” should be explicit because it changes the relationship with users. It removes the incentive to trap manuscripts, meter analysis, or turn editorial anxiety into recurring revenue. If support or donations ever exist, they must not create a lesser product for people who do not pay.
13

What success looks like

Better questions, earlier

  • A novelist catches a broken payoff before querying
  • An editor verifies a continuity concern in minutes
  • A revision report explains what changed across versions
  • A team can disagree while looking at the same evidence
Success is not the number of flags produced. It is useful editorial decisions: a caught contradiction, a clarified intention, a strengthened setup, or a false positive dismissed quickly because the evidence was visible. We should measure precision, recall on seeded problems, time saved, and whether users can understand why a finding exists.

Invitation

Bring us the hard manuscripts

We want real revision histories, known continuity traps, and candid editorial judgment about what helps or distracts.

  • Authors willing to test on their own work
  • Editors who can grade findings
  • Messy PDFs and unusual structures
  • Known problems for repeatable evaluation
  • Workflows we have not imagined
The next step is not promotion at scale. It is exposure to difficult, varied books and people with strong editorial instincts. We need test manuscripts where the author or editor already knows some of the traps, plus permission to learn from the workflow without collecting the manuscript itself.
