Bookinator 0.1.0

The book is larger than a prompt

A working local editorial system that reads by chapter, remembers across the manuscript, and shows what produced its conclusions.

Bookinator began with a simple frustration. A manuscript can be coherent one chapter at a time and still fail across the whole book. Version 0.1.0 now demonstrates a complete local workflow on a real manuscript. This deck shows what works, where it helps, and where the product still has to earn trust.
01

The editing problem

Distant details create expensive questions

A promise, location, relationship, or rule can return hundreds of pages after it first appeared.

Page 30The book establishes something smallPage 450The story depends on it
Authors and editors build notebooks, spreadsheets, search rituals, and margin notes because working memory cannot hold every detail. Search remains essential, but it depends on knowing which word to search. The harder questions involve meaning, recurrence, absence, and what changed between distant passages.

The working loop

Bookinator separates production work from presentation. Analysis creates durable evidence. Whole-book jobs reconcile only the saved child artifacts they need. Explore turns those results into useful editorial views. Reports can be regenerated without asking the model to reread the manuscript.
04

Analysis

One manuscript, several independent lenses

A failed or weak analysis does not erase the work that succeeded.

ChaptersAccepted structure and source spans
SummariesWhat happened and what remains open
DossiersFacts, events, entities, questions, and evidence
Emotions and TagsChapter-scale shape and editorial signals
SmellsSource-linked sentences for human review
InferencesSlower algorithms across the whole book
Bookinator does not ask one model for a universal opinion of the book. Each lens has its own status, saved result, model provenance, and failure behavior. That division makes long local work restartable and lets the editor decide which kinds of evidence matter.
05

Book-scale memory

Chapter records become whole-book views

Summaries provide navigation. Dossiers preserve smaller facts and questions that a summary would smooth away.

Chapter evidenceSaved independently
Whole-book rollupsReconcile current artifacts
InferencesCompare evidence across chapters
ExploreMake the result inspectable
The distinction between summaries and dossiers matters. Summaries are readable maps of the story. Dossiers retain structured observations and evidence. Whole-book rollups combine the chapter work, while separate inference jobs produce Connections and Questions & Payoffs.
06

Summaries and dossiers

Orientation without discarding the small things

Summary

A readable account of the chapter and a fast route through the book.

Dossier

Structured entities, locations, events, facts, questions, promises, timeline observations, and supporting evidence.

Rollup

A whole-book synthesis built after the required chapter artifacts are current.

These records are useful before Bookinator identifies any problem. They let an editor move through an unfamiliar manuscript quickly and ask better questions. Dossier evidence still varies in precision. Exact durable anchors for every claim remain future work.
07

Connections

Who appears where, and with what else

Bookinator groups dossier forms into inspectable entities, locations, and time observations.

QuestionCurrent view
Where does Yellowstone appear?Sortable chapters and mentions
Who shares those scenes?Same-section and same-chapter intersections
Where is a character absent?Distinctive no-presence markers
Did two names get merged?Forms, aliases, and close-name suggestions
Connections is valuable because absence can matter as much as presence. An editor can inspect aliases and close names rather than accepting silent merges. Grouping is still heuristic. Misspellings, pluralization, and entity-versus-location errors require review, and that review should become durable in a later release.
08

Questions & Payoffs

Follow the question, not only the wording

A book-level inference groups retellings, records where the question arose, and proposes where it may have been answered.

RaisedChapter 3

This is one of the first genuinely book-scale tools. The table can sort by status, source chapter, resolution chapter, and chapter distance. The matches remain hypotheses. An editor can inspect the question, its retellings, and the proposed answer rather than trusting the label alone.
09

Story shape

Emotion, tags, and Smells answer different questions

Emotion mapChapter length and emotions above a chosen threshold
TagsTense, horror, identity, point of view, setup, and other editorial signals
SmellsSource-linked sentences that survived deterministic detection and model review
The Emotion map reveals large-scale movement while keeping chapter titles and word counts visible. Tags describe chapter-level editorial characteristics. Smells operate at sentence level and support dismissal. None of these is a quality score. They are different ways to decide where an editor may want to look.
10

Inspectable pipeline

Waiting is visible. Failure stays local.

Long local model jobs expose their state instead of disappearing behind one spinner.

WaitingEligible work is queued
RunningModel, start time, duration, and input size
CompleteA usable artifact was saved
FailedThe attempt and diagnostic remain inspectable
Local models can take minutes per chapter. Bookinator records enough detail to tell slow work from stuck work and a failed artifact from a failed application. Completed chapters remain complete. A retry should affect the failed job, not erase the rest of the book.

Current boundaries

Grouping can be wrong. Timeline observations remain rough. Source precision varies by artifact. Large models are slow, and malformed model output still needs careful recovery. The product should name these limits rather than borrow credibility from work that remains on the roadmap.
14

The next serious work

Human review and stronger evidence anchors

The next milestone should make editorial decisions durable, portable, and safer across revisions.

  • Reviewer annotations and dispositions
  • Stable source ranges for dossier claims
  • Explicit, reversible grouping decisions
  • Public-domain and consented evaluation books
  • Faster checkpointed inference
Bookinator now produces enough analysis that the human layer matters most. Reviewers need to accept, dismiss, annotate, and export decisions without losing them to a rerun. Better anchors and evaluation will also make the cross-book inferences more trustworthy.
